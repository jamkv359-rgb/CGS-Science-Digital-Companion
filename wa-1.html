The Universal Solvent

"Water, water, everywhere, / Nor any drop to drink." (Samuel Taylor Coleridge, The Rime of the Ancient Mariner)

Learning Intentions and Success Criteria

Learning intentionSuccess criteria (knowledge)Success criteria (skills)
LI1: The structure of water and its properties (1 week)Define and explain the structure of the water molecule, focusing on covalent bonding. List and describe water's "unusual" properties: polarity, solubility, specific heat capacity, cohesion, adhesion, surface tension and wetting. Explain polarity and hydrogen bonding.Apply covalent bonding to other molecules. Explain changes to surface tension via polarity and detergents. Relate the properties of water to conditions on Earth.
LI2: Acids and bases, their properties and reactions (2 weeks)Define acids as sources of H+ and alkalis as sources of OH−; acids donate protons, bases accept them. List common acids (sulfuric, nitric, hydrochloric, ethanoic) and bases (sodium, potassium and calcium hydroxide, sodium carbonate) with formulas. Describe their properties. Use litmus, phenolphthalein and methyl orange. Use the pH scale (0–14) and universal indicator. Recall the reactions of acids with bases, oxides, carbonates, hydrogen carbonates and ammonia solution.Carry out simple titrations to compare household cleaners. Use indicators to find the pH of unknowns. Predict products and write balanced equations.
LI3: Solubility tables and graphs (1 week)Define solubility. Use a solubility table to predict precipitation. Describe unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated solutions on a solubility graph.Observe precipitation reactions. Perform simple concentration calculations using solubility curves.
LI4: Qualitative analysis of cations and anions (1 week)Identify unknown ionic compounds using tests for anions and cations.Identify Li+, Na+, K+, Ca2+, Ba2+, Cu2+ (flame tests); NH4+ (NaOH, ammonia evolved); Cu2+, Fe2+, Fe3+ (NaOH, then KSCN and K3Fe(CN)6); Cl−, Br−, I− (dilute HNO3 and AgNO3); SO42− (dilute HCl and BaCl2); CO32− (dilute HCl, CO2 evolved).

Summative assessment tasks: Water Analysis Test; Investigating cations and anions or Water Assignment.

The Science Toolbox

A scientist uses many tools to answer scientific questions: diagrams (pencil, simple 2D, labelled), experimentation (fair tests), flow charts, graphs (labelled axes with units), maps, photographs, surveys and tables. Bring this workbook, a protractor, calculator, ruler, pens, pencil, eraser and your laptop.
