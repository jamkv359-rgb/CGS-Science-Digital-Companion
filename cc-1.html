Did you know?

"The great pleasure and feeling in my right brain is more than my left brain can find words to tell you." (Roger Sperry)

1.1 Learning Intentions and Assessments

Learning intentionSuccess criteria (knowledge)Success criteria (skills)
1. Understand that food, water and oxygen are essential for survival.Recall the key nutrients required for life. Briefly explain the importance of food, water and oxygen to cells and organisms.
2. Define diffusion, how it moves nutrients and wastes into and out of cells, and why cells need to be small.Define diffusion. Explain diffusion and osmosis across a semipermeable membrane. Explain how nutrients diffuse into cells and wastes diffuse out. Compare unicellular and multicellular life. Understand the limits of unicellular life and how multicellular organisms overcome them.Use diagrams to show diffusion and osmosis across a semipermeable membrane. Investigate the relationship between surface area to volume ratio and rate of diffusion.
3. The structure and function of the nervous system, including the brain and reflex arcs.Describe the CNS and PNS. Describe the structure of a neuron. Identify the three types of neuron. Outline the autonomic nervous system and its sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions. Understand the reflex arc and how it minimises harm.Examine the structure and function of the brain. Draw and label a reflex arc.
4. The structure and function of the endocrine system, including glands and hormones.Summarise the endocrine system. Identify hormones as chemical messengers and explain how they reach target cells. Compare the hormonal and nervous systems.Explain the structure and function of one hormone in detail.
5. Homeostasis and how the body maintains its internal state.Define homeostasis and explain why a steady internal state is important. Apply the Stimulus-Response Model. Explain glucose control and how it goes wrong in diabetes.Draw and interpret stimulus-response diagrams.

Summative assessment: Hormones assignment; Coordination and Control test.
Formative assessment: DEEDS quizzes; booklet checks; practical tasks (cell size limits and diffusion, brain examination, temperature and homeostasis).
