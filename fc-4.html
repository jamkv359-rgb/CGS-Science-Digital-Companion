Balancing chemical equations

Balancing chemical equations makes sure the law of conservation of mass is upheld: the atoms in the reactants are rearranged into the products, so there must be the same number of each type of atom on both sides.

Exercise: balancing equations (code puzzle)

Balance each equation, writing in all the coefficients (including 1). Then find each equation's sequence of coefficients in the key list and write down its letter. Reading the letters in order spells a message! Hint: treat polyatomic ions like NO3−, PO43− and SO42− as one object on both sides.

Exercise: putting it all together

  1. Write the chemical formulas first. Once they are correct, don't change them.
  2. Balance the atoms on each side by placing numbers (coefficients) in front of each formula.
  3. Treat polyatomic ions such as NO3−, PO43− and SO42− as a group on both sides.
  4. If you have an even number of an atom on one side and an odd number on the other, double everything and continue.
