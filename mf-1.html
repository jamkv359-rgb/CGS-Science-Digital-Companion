Chapter 1

Overview of Motion and Forces

In this chapter (2 sections)
  1. 1.1 Learning Intentions and Success Criteria
  2. 1.2 Timeline
Did you know?

"In the dance of particles, unseen, / Force whispers secrets, yet keen. / A push, a pull, a cosmic embrace, / Motion unfolds, in time and space." (C. G. P. Tran)

1.1 Learning Intentions and Success Criteria

Learning intentionSuccess criteria (knowledge)Success criteria (skills)
(1) Explore motion and the different types of motion, e.g. constant speed, speeding up or slowing down.Recall that a change in position is displacement, displacement in a given time is velocity, and velocity change in a given time is acceleration. Understand that velocity and acceleration are rates of change.Apply these descriptions to the motion of vehicles, animals, races, sports and hunts. Calculate displacement, velocity and acceleration.
(2) Discover how motion can be represented in graphs.Know and interpret displacement–time and velocity–time graphs (constant acceleration: zero or non-zero).Create motion graphs. Analyse them to decide whether an object is stationary, moving away or back, moving at constant speed, speeding up or slowing down.
(3) Investigate forces, the types of forces and their effects.Force is a push, pull or twist; contact or non-contact. Non-contact interactions: gravity, electricity and magnetism. Contact forces: normal force, friction, tension and fluid pressure. A force can speed up, slow down or change the direction of an object, or change its shape.Use forces to find mass or measure force. Identify contact and non-contact forces. Evaluate sliding friction and air resistance and the factors that affect them.
(4) Determine how forces on an object give no net force (balanced) or a net force (unbalanced).Two or more forces may act at once, giving either no net force or a net force.Identify balanced and unbalanced situations.
(5) Investigate the relationship between net force, mass and acceleration.A net force causes acceleration, directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass.Measure acceleration from an applied net force; evaluate the effect of changing the force or the mass.
(6) Discover how forces occur in pairs acting on pairs of objects.The force by one body on another equals the force by the second on the first, in the opposite direction.Apply Newton's third law to identify and evaluate force pairs.
