Learning Intentions and Success Criteria

Learning intentionSuccess criteria (knowledge)Success criteria (skills)
History and importance of metals.Recall the key periods of metal use, from prehistoric to modern. Understand native metals, ores, and the role of copper, bronze, iron and steel.Explain the journey of metals from the earth to human use. Assess the significance of metals in Australia.
The alkali metals.Recall the six alkali metals and the three used in school labs. Understand their physical properties and high reactivity, their ionic compounds and flame colours.Predict alkali metal reactions; explain reactivity using the single valence electron; evaluate safety measures.
Trends in Group 1.Recall trends in softness, density, melting and boiling points; understand why reactivity increases down the group.Use atomic size, electron loss and nuclear attraction to explain the trend.
Group 1 vs Group 2.Recall differences in reactivity, hardness, melting point and reaction with water; Group 1 lose 1 electron, Group 2 lose 2.Use electron configuration to explain and compare reactivity.
Structure of metals and metallic bonding.Recall the lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons. Link properties to structure. Compare metallic and ionic bonding.Explain malleability, ductility, conductivity and melting points; explain why ionic compounds are brittle.
Alloys.Define an alloy; recall brass, steel and bronze; understand substitutional and interstitial alloys.Explain how atom arrangement changes properties; compare pure metals and alloys.
Reactions of metals.Recall: metal + oxygen → metal oxide; metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen; metal + acid → salt + hydrogen. Understand displacement reactions.Use the reactivity series to predict reactions and products; write balanced equations.
Extraction of metals.Define oxidation and reduction; recall oxidising and reducing agents; understand electrolysis and the blast furnace.Write half-equations; predict electrolysis products; choose the right extraction method using the reactivity series.

Summative assessment tasks: Minerals to Metals Test; Practical Test or Investigation.

The Science Toolbox

A scientist uses many tools to answer scientific questions: diagrams (pencil, simple 2D, labelled), experimentation (fair tests), flow charts, graphs (labelled axes with units), maps, photographs, surveys and tables. Bring this workbook, a protractor, calculator, ruler, pens, pencil, eraser and your laptop.
